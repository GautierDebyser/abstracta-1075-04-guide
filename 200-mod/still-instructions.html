2. Understand bones and connector orientation

A bone is one tube with an inserted connector at each end. Select bones A–L on the plan to inspect their final directions before assembly. A and L use two 3T connectors each; B–K use two 4T connectors each. At the base, unused vertical prongs point upward. Prongs insert inside the tubes and shoulders meet the tube ends flush.

3. Prepare twelve base bones A–L

Fit the twenty-four bottom connectors to twelve 15.08″ #395 depth tubes. First align both connectors with two prongs resting on a wood block. Check their directions; partially seat, then tap flush using the 913 nylon mallet. During tapping, the lower connector and tube end are supported end-on by the block. Turn end-for-end and repeat. The mallet and seating travel are illustrative.

5. Join the eleven-bay base ladder

Fit two joining tubes onto A’s inward prongs, one front and one rear. Bring receiving bone B onto both free ends together and seat progressively with the 913. Then bring C through L onto their joining pairs in order. Receiving bones move rigidly; the animation does not insert a full-length tube between two immovable connectors. The base uses 34 tubes: twelve depth bones and twenty-two joining tubes. Work on a protected surface and support joints.
